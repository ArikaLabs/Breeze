Everything the remote does, without finding the remote.

The remote, where your cursor is

Temperature, mode, fan and airflow in one panel, and several units under one icon. The set point sits beside the clock, so you know the room without opening anything.

It talks to the unit, not to a server

Commands cross your Wi-Fi and land in milliseconds. Nothing about your home leaves it, and the app keeps working when Midea's cloud goes down.

Say it, or schedule it

"Hey Siri, start cooling with Breeze." Every control is a Shortcuts action and a breeze:// link, so the room can cool itself when you sit down and rest when you leave.

Reads the room

Temperature and humidity from the sensors inside the unit sit in the header, beside the power it is drawing right now.

Energy, logged

Live watts and the unit's own kilowatt-hour counter, kept in a log on your Mac. Today, this week, all time.

Knows when your Mac runs hot

Warm chip, warm room, air conditioner off: the menu bar icon changes colour and offers to start cooling. Nothing happens without you.

Before you download.

Does it need an account or the internet?

Once. During setup Breeze signs in to the MSmartHome account your unit is paired with, fetches the unit's local key, and forgets your password. From then on it only talks to the unit over your Wi-Fi. No analytics, no telemetry, no account with us.

macOS says the app cannot be opened.

Breeze is not yet notarized by Apple, so the first launch is blocked. Click Done (macOS 15) or Cancel (macOS 14), then within the hour open System Settings → Privacy & Security and choose Open Anyway. On macOS 14 you can instead Control-click Breeze and choose Open. It happens once. In Terminal, this does the same:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Breeze.app
Will it work with my unit?

If it is a Midea-made air conditioner with Wi-Fi and the MSmartHome app controls it, yes. Units sold under other names on the same module should work but have not been tried here. See the list of models.

Is it free?

Free, and open source under the MIT license. The code, the issues and every release are on GitHub.

Will it work with my unit?

Breeze speaks the local protocol of the Wi-Fi module Midea has built into its air conditioners since about 2019, sold under its own name and a dozen others. One condition: the unit must be paired in the MSmartHome app, because Midea's cloud hands the local key only to the account that owns it.

A quick check. When you set the unit up, it broadcast a Wi-Fi network called net_ac_XXXX, and MSmartHome controls it today. If both are true, Breeze will find it.
